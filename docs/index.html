From one aggregated survey to a validated people-analytics pipeline

A fintech client wanted to know what drives employee engagement and who is at risk of disengaging, but its survey held only department averages. I diagnosed what the real data could show, validated the methods on calibrated synthetic data, and turned the gap into a 2026 survey redesign.

The dashboard

A Streamlit app that explains the results to non-technical readers. Every page opens with its key takeaways.

Hosted free on Streamlit Community Cloud. If nobody has opened it recently, it takes a moment to wake up.

Four questions, four pages

Each page leads with its conclusion, and every chart is labelled with its data source.

1 of 4 · Where does engagement stand?

The gaps are between departments, not themes

Rebuilt absolute scores from a workbook that stored each department as a difference from the company mean, and put 1–5 and 1–10 questions on one scale. A department tends to be strong or weak across the board, and two of the client's own theme scores turned out to be misleading.

2 of 4 · Where should the client act?

Priority questions split into company-wide and localised problems, each needing a different fix

All 34 questions placed by their score and by how much departments disagree on them. Clustering found no distinct groups of departments, and the page says so instead of forcing segments.

3 of 4 · What drives engagement, and who is at risk? · Synthetic data, proof of method

Tested on data with a known answer, the pipeline found it

1,000 employees × 3 survey waves, calibrated to the real survey, with two quality checks that stop the run if the data drifts. Regression-based driver analysis recovered all 3 drivers built into the data. The best of three risk classifiers reached 0.79 ROC-AUC and caught 78% of at-risk employees on a wave it never saw, with SHAP showing what drives each score.

4 of 4 · What should the 2026 survey collect?

Extend the survey, don't replace it

Five client questions failed for one reason: the data was never collected. The redesign has 11 changes (6 add, 2 keep, 2 fix, 1 drop), each traced back to the analysis behind it. In the demonstration, almost half of what drives engagement is not asked in today's survey.

  • Scope before code: four client objectives refined into four answerable ones, each with a stated evidence base (real, synthetic or both).
  • Quality gates that stop the run: if the synthetic data drifts from the real survey or loses its research-based relationships, the pipeline fails rather than warns.
  • Every chart is labelled REAL, SYNTHETIC or DEMO, enforced in code, so a public copy can never pass demo numbers off as client data.
  • Reproducible: one command rebuilds every figure and table in about 2 minutes; pytest and GitHub Actions run on every push.
